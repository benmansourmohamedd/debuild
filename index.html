I’m Mohamed Benmansour, a student, designer, and researcher working across architecture, computation, and social inquiry.

My research examines how governance, cultural knowledge, and technical systems become spatial rules. My work combines fieldwork, writing, mapping, archival research, and computational design.

Below are selected projects and papers. For a comprehensive record of research, talks, education, and experience, see my CV.

ALGORITHMIC THRESHOLDS OF ACCESS (research series)

paper ↗ request dossier ↗

This ongoing project traces how eligibility, waiting, and intake procedures become spatial systems. Field notes document queues, counters, forms, time windows, partitions, and points where access can fail.

Rather than treating these environments as neutral, the research reads the built world as procedural code: a system that sorts bodies and distributes access unevenly.

Washington, DC · 2025–ongoing · field research, mapping, writing

THE CORRIDOR IS AN ALGORITHM (comparative field study)

related paper ↗ project notes ↗

A comparative study of institutional interiors focused on routing, sightlines, checkpoints, waiting nodes, staff-only boundaries, and the spatial habits that precede direct instruction.

The project combines annotated plans, photographic documentation, and writing to understand circulation as a form of governance.

Baltimore, Maryland · 2024–2025 · spatial analysis and field documentation

ETHNOMATHEMATICS OF REPAIR (archive and methods project)

methods paper ↗ archive notes ↗

An archive of informal repair practices that reads measurement, fit, tolerance, sequence, and improvisation as situated mathematical knowledge.

The work asks how expertise produced through necessity and care can be documented without flattening it into institutional categories.

DC–Baltimore region · 2024–ongoing · archive, protocol, and interviews

deBUILD (research archive and publishing system)

project index ↗

A computational archive for comparable socio-spatial casework. Each entry joins observation, analysis, diagrams, images, and references in a stable structure.

The website is treated as research infrastructure rather than a promotional surface: a place where methods remain visible and knowledge can accumulate over time.

Digital · 2023–ongoing · editorial system, web design, and research
