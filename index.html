Computational + Socio-Spatial Research

Architecture at the intersection of computation, ethnomathematics, anthropology, and society.

Mohamed Benmansour is a student, designer, and researcher working across computation, algorithms, ethnomathematics, anthropology, sociology, and architecture. His work investigates architecture as both a computational and social system, examining how mathematical structures, cultural knowledge, governance, and lived experience become encoded into space. His research approaches these intersections through a decolonial lens.

Research

Benmansour approaches architecture as a socio-technical system in which computational logic, mathematical knowledge, cultural practice, institutional power, and lived experience continuously produce one another.

Computation + Algorithms

Computational visualization, modeling, simulation, algorithmic systems, generative processes, and spatial computation as both design methods and analytical instruments.

Ethnomathematics

Cultural mathematical systems, spatial reasoning, pattern, measurement, informal knowledge, and mathematical epistemologies situated outside supposedly universal models.

Anthropology + Sociology

Lived environments, cultural practices, social organization, institutions, governance, and relationships between people, knowledge, and built space.

Architecture

Architecture as both research object and medium: the spatial condition through which mathematical, computational, political, and cultural systems become material.

Decolonial Inquiry

Examination of which systems of architectural and mathematical knowledge become institutionalized, which are marginalized, and how alternative epistemologies can inform spatial practice.

Socio-Spatial Systems

Housing, institutional environments, carceral infrastructures, informal environments, survival infrastructures, governance, access, and spatial manifestations of power.
